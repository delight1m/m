The problem

Why good books stay invisible

Most books that struggle on Amazon are well written. The gap is usually in the details that decide who sees the cover in the first place, and those details are rarely explained to the author.

1

Categories chosen once

Many authors pick categories at upload and never revisit them, even after the book has grown into a different readership or a sharper sub-genre.

2

Keywords written for the author

Keywords often describe the book in the author's own language. Readers search with different words, and that gap quietly costs visibility every day.

3

Descriptions that tell but do not sell

A description can summarise the story perfectly and still give a browsing reader no reason to click Buy within the first few seconds.

01 · Core service

Discoverability and Amazon Strategy

This is the foundation. If readers who are actively searching for a book like yours cannot find it, the rest of your marketing has to work much harder to compensate. This is where I start with every author, and it is where every engagement begins.

The problem most authors do not see

Most indie books are set up with the wrong categories, weak or missing keywords, and metadata that does not match how readers actually search. The result is an invisible book. Your book may be exactly what a reader wants, but if the metadata is not speaking that reader's language, Amazon has no reliable way to connect the two of you.

Amazon hosts an enormous number of titles competing for the same attention. A listing that is not structured to compete slips down the results, not because the book is weak, but because the system cannot see it clearly.

What I actually do

I audit and rebuild your Amazon presence from the ground up. That covers every category, every keyword slot, every backend metadata field, your A+ Content and your advertising strategy. Each element is set up to work with the others, as one system.

Everything is grounded in research into how readers in your specific genre browse and search. Nothing is left on default settings, and nothing is copied from a generic playbook. Each choice is made with your book in mind and can be explained.

How I do it

I use keyword research tools to see the phrases readers type and how strongly they are used, and I cross-check those findings against manual review of competing titles and Amazon's own search suggestions. I look at what is ranking, why it is ranking, and which gaps your book is well placed to fill.

There is no guesswork and no "let us try this and see what happens". Every recommendation has evidence behind it and reasoning that I can explain to you in plain English.

What you get

A book positioned to be found by the readers most likely to buy it. Stronger category placement, better keyword visibility, and advertising that points readers to a listing that works. The foundation makes every other marketing effort you make more effective.

You also receive the strategy documents, keyword lists and research files as PDF, DOCX and editable spreadsheets. They are yours to keep, whether or not we work together again.

What is included in this service

Every discoverability engagement includes these core deliverables, tailored to your book and genre.

  • Category research and selection. Amazon and BISAC category mapping, with placement requests made where the platform allows.

  • Amazon Ads setup and strategy. Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display campaigns planned and built from the start.
  • A+ Content creation. Module design and copy, either implemented for you or supplied so your designer can build it.

  • Series page optimisation. For series, your series page rebuilt to encourage read-through and discovery.
  • Author Central setup. Your author profile checked and optimised across the regional Amazon stores you sell in.
  • Countdown deal strategy. Promotional pricing timed to support visibility and sales momentum, with the reasons recorded.
  • Read-through analysis. For series authors, a review of where readers drop off and what to change.
  • Ongoing category review. Categories reassessed regularly, because rankings and reader interest shift over time.
  • Written report and documentation. Everything recorded in PDF, DOCX and editable spreadsheets that you keep.
02 · Launch and promotion

Launch and Promotion

Your launch window is when the algorithm pays the most attention to a new title. A coordinated launch can set the course for months, sometimes years, of sales. A weak launch can bury a book that might otherwise have done well.

The problem with how most launches happen

Many indie authors publish first and only start thinking about marketing afterwards. By the time things are organised, the most useful part of the launch window has gone. The book sits in the new-release stream and starts from behind.

Others front-load excitement without a plan. A burst of sales in week one, mostly from friends and family, is followed by a sharp drop when the initial interest fades and nothing sustainable is in place.

What a proper launch looks like

I build a launch timeline that brings together pre-orders, advance reader copies, promo site submissions, advertising ramp-up and release-day activity. The aim is to create momentum that carries past the first week.

Every launch is planned backward from release day, usually starting eight to twelve weeks before publication. Each task has a date and an owner, and everything is written down, so nothing depends on memory.

How launch coordination works

I plan advance reader copy distribution to encourage early honest reviews, and I research promotional sites that suit your genre and budget before booking anything. Pre-order pricing and duration are chosen for your situation, not copied from a template.

Advertising is set up before launch and ramped up on release day. Newsletter swaps and cross-promotion opportunities are identified, and the whole plan is timed to build momentum through the first thirty to sixty days.

What you get from a proper launch

A launch that feels organised rather than chaotic. A realistic chance of reviews around release day. Sales activity in week one that supports the listing, and a clear plan for weeks two, three and four, rather than release day and hope.

Most importantly, you get a launch you can carry out without losing your sense of what comes next. Everything is broken into small tasks with deadlines.

What is included in launch services

  • Custom launch timeline. An 8 to 12 week plan built around your book, genre and goals.
  • ARC strategy and coordination. Advance reader copy strategy, team building and distribution management.
  • Pre-order setup and optimisation. Pricing, positioning and duration chosen for your book.
  • Promo site research and bookings. Suitable promotional sites researched, compared and booked to fit your genre and budget.
  • Release day coordination. A complete release day checklist so nothing is forgotten.
  • Review acquisition strategy. Post-launch approaches to earning honest reviews, within platform rules.
  • Newsletter swap coordination. Cross-promotion arrangements with other authors in your genre.
  • Weekly performance reports. Sales, rankings, ad performance and next steps, reported in plain language.
03 · Content and visibility

Content and Visibility

Once readers find your book, it has to convert them. Your cover, your blurb and your author presence all shape whether a click becomes a sale, and whether that sale becomes a reader who comes back for the next book.

Where great metadata still loses sales

A book can have excellent metadata and still lose sales. The cover may not fit the expectations of its genre, the blurb may describe the book instead of selling it, or there may be no author presence to build trust with a browsing reader.

This is where authors often overspend without improving results. A beautiful cover can signal the wrong genre. A blurb can read like a summary. An elaborate email system can be abandoned after three months because it was never sustainable.

How I approach content work

I give honest feedback on your cover. Does it match the conventions of your sub-genre? Does it read clearly at thumbnail size? Does it promise the kind of book you have written? I then write or refine your blurb with a structure built around a hook, the central conflict and the stakes.

I help you set up the author presence that supports long-term visibility without becoming a full-time job: a website, an email list and social channels, in the minimum useful form.

The craft behind this work

Cover feedback is grounded in the study of top-performing books in your sub-genre. Blurbs are built on a hook, a conflict and stakes, and then tested against how readers in your genre actually scan a listing. Romance blurbs read differently from thriller blurbs, which read differently from literary ones.

Website and email guidance is deliberately practical. The goal is to focus on what moves the needle, and to say plainly when something can be skipped.

What you walk away with

A professional, genre-appropriate package that turns interest into purchases. A blurb that reads the way your ideal reader hopes a book will read. Clear guidance on which author platform pieces you need, and explicit permission to ignore the ones you do not.

Every piece of content, whether a blurb, a cover report or a website structure guide, is delivered as editable DOCX and PDF files that you can keep and reuse.

Content services included

  • Cover feedback and positioning. Genre fit analysis, thumbnail testing and honest notes on what works and what does not.
  • Blurb writing or optimisation. A full rewrite or careful refinement using structures that work in your genre.
  • Book description editorial review. Line-by-line polish of your Amazon description for clarity and impact.
  • Author website structure. Which pages you need, what belongs on each, and what to leave out entirely.
  • Email list strategy. Setup guidance, a welcome sequence outline and a content rhythm that fits your life.
  • Newsletter template creation. Ready-to-use templates you can populate quickly for regular reader contact.
  • Social media direction. Which platforms matter for your genre, what to post and what to skip.
  • Back matter optimisation. End-of-book content designed to encourage the next read, reviews and newsletter sign-ups.
04 · PR and outreach

PR and Outreach

Readers do not only discover books through Amazon. Podcasts, blogs, press coverage and social creators all introduce books to people who would never have searched for them. This service puts your book in front of those readers, on the platforms they already trust.

Why most author pitches are ignored

Pitching press, podcasts and bloggers takes knowing who to contact, what each outlet covers, what makes a story worth their time, and how to write something they will answer. Most authors send one generic message to a long list and hear nothing back.

They often conclude that PR does not work. In reality, the pitch was never opened, or it was opened, recognised as generic and deleted within seconds.

How real outreach works

Every campaign starts with research rather than a mailing list. I identify the podcasts your readers listen to, the bloggers they trust and the journalists who cover books like yours, and I note what each one has published recently.

Pitches are written individually and tied to a specific hook that matters to that outlet. Follow-ups are timed sensibly, and every response is tracked so the campaign can improve over time.

The research behind every pitch

Before anything is sent, I look at who covers books like yours, what their audience looks like, what hooks are most likely to interest them, and how they prefer to be contacted. Outreach to social creators follows each platform's rules, with clear disclosure where it is required.

The coverage you actually want

Coverage in outlets your readers really use: podcast guest appearances in front of engaged listeners, blog features and reviews that remain useful for years, and media kit assets you can reuse across your marketing. Real relationships with people who may cover your next book too.

Done well, PR builds value across your whole author career rather than producing a single burst of attention.

PR and outreach services

  • Press release writing. Professional releases written to be opened, read and covered.
  • Podcast guest pitching. Targeted pitches to podcasts your specific readers listen to.
  • Book blogger outreach. Research and outreach to reviewers, bloggers and content creators in your genre.
  • Bookstagram and BookTok outreach. Creator identification and outreach in the visual and video book communities.
  • Media kit creation. A single press kit with what journalists and podcast hosts need.
  • Author bio and press photo guidance. Bio writing and clear direction for a press photo that suits your genre.
  • Interview preparation. Talking points, story angles and preparation notes for podcast and press appearances.

05 · Sales and distribution

Sales and Distribution

Amazon is not the only place readers buy books. A considered wider strategy opens new revenue streams and reduces dependence on one platform, which matters when a single algorithm change can reduce income almost overnight.

The Amazon dependency problem

Many indie authors are exclusive to Amazon by default rather than by decision. They miss readers on other retailers, in libraries and in audio, and they have no direct channel to readers. When something changes on the platform, they have no backup and little leverage.

This is not an argument against Amazon. For many books it will remain the largest source of income. The risk lies in building an entire business on one platform you do not control.

The wide or exclusive decision

I assess whether going wide makes sense for your book, your genre and your business goals. Not every author should go wide. For some genres and business models, a focused exclusive strategy is the better choice, and the decision should rest on evidence rather than fear or fashion.

If wide makes sense, I set up distribution through the platforms that suit your book. If it does not, I will tell you honestly and we will focus your resources where they matter most.

How distribution setup works

Every distribution choice is grounded in your genre, your series structure and your goals. Once we agree the direction, I handle the technical setup on each platform: print distribution for bookstores and libraries, wide ebook distribution, audio production and library platforms, each set up correctly the first time.

Building a resilient author business

A distribution strategy that fits your book and your business, a direct sales route that captures readers and builds long-term value, and reporting that shows what is actually working across every channel.

You also get a consolidated view of sales across platforms, so decisions are based on your own numbers.

Distribution services included

  • Wide versus exclusive strategy. A data-led assessment of whether going wide suits your situation.
  • Print distribution setup. IngramSpark account setup and optimisation for bookstore and library reach.
  • Wide ebook distribution. Setup through Draft2Digital or Smashwords, as suits your book.
  • Library distribution. Setup for OverDrive, Hoopla and other library platforms where appropriate.
  • Audiobook strategy. A comparison of ACX and Findaway Voices, with setup for the option you choose.
  • Direct sales funnel. Setup of a direct-to-reader store such as Payhip, Shopify or BookFunnel, for better margins.
  • Multi-platform pricing strategy. Pricing coordinated across platforms for revenue and reader reach.
  • Sales tracking dashboard. Consolidated reporting across all platforms in one clear view.

A note on expectations

Why I tell the hard truth

Many book marketers make dramatic claims with no context, because a big promise is an easy way to get a call. I do not work that way, and I want you to know what to expect before we begin.

Results depend heavily on the book itself. The best discoverability strategy cannot rescue weak writing, poor editing or a cover that misrepresents the book. My job is to make your book fully visible. Your job, and the book's, is to make a reader want to buy it.

Every genre performs differently

Genres

Genres I work in, and how I approach each one

Every genre has its own reader habits, shelf logic and cover expectations. My analysis starts with the readers, not with a template, and each genre is treated on its own terms.

Genres I work in

Twelve genre groups

Mystery and Thriller

Tension, pacing and sub-genre signals matter here. I look at how readers separate cosy mysteries, procedurals and psychological thrillers, and make sure your categories, keywords and blurb point to the right group.

Romance

Romance readers shop by trope and heat level as much as by genre. I help you name your tropes clearly, choose sub-categories with the right readers, and align the cover and blurb with the promise you make.

Science Fiction

Sci-fi readers are specific. Hard science, military, space opera and cyberpunk each attract different buyers. I position your book with the readership most likely to enjoy it, and compare it with established titles in that lane.

Fantasy

Epic, urban, low fantasy and romantasy each have distinct browsing habits. I work on sub-genre clarity, series sequencing and keywords that match how fantasy readers search.

Children's Fiction

Parents usually search, and children usually read. I write for both audiences, with age and reading-level signals for the adult buyer and a listing that appeals to the young reader.

Young Adult

YA listings must speak to teenagers and to the adults who influence what they buy. I review audience framing, cover conventions and blurb tone for both.

Horror and Paranormal

Tone is the key signal. I help you separate gore, psychological horror and supernatural romance, so the right fans find the book and the wrong ones do not.

Literary and Contemporary Fiction

Quieter books need precise positioning. I help you describe the book honestly, choose comparison titles with care and avoid vague copy that hides the premise.

Historical Fiction

Period, setting and point of view all shape the reader. I look at period keywords, comparison titles and how the blurb signals the era and the emotional stakes.

Non-Fiction

Non-fiction is bought to solve a problem or answer a question. I focus on subtitle clarity, problem-led descriptions and the categories where your reader actually looks.

Memoir and Biography

A personal story still needs a reason to be chosen now. I help you balance the human story with a clear hook, and position the book for readers who want that kind of account.

Not listed? Just ask

If your genre is not on this list, send a message. I will tell you honestly whether I can help, and what I would look at first.

General

What do you do?
I help authors make their books easier to find on Amazon and beyond, through categories, keywords, metadata, launch planning, advertising, content review, outreach and distribution guidance.
Who do you work with?
Indie and hybrid authors, debut and established writers, series and standalone authors, and non-fiction and memoir writers who sell through Amazon or plan to.
Do you work with authors outside the United States?
Yes. I am based in New York and work with authors worldwide. Time zones are handled through messages and email, so location is rarely a barrier.
Which platforms do you cover?
Amazon is the main focus. For wide distribution, I also help you weigh other retailers, library channels and audiobook options, and set them up where they suit your book.
Do I need to be self-published?
Not necessarily. What matters is that you manage, or can influence, your book's listing and promotions. If that describes you, we can talk.

The process

How does the process begin?
You send your book details through the contact form or by email, or start with the free book audit. I reply with the next steps and, once you agree, begin the written audit.
How long does the audit take?
Usually about 5 to 7 business days after your intake details arrive. Complex or multi-book requests may take longer, and I will tell you upfront.
Will I have to make the changes myself?
That depends on the package. Audit Only is a plan you carry out, with my support for questions. Setup and Launch and Full Service Partnership include work I carry out as agreed, with every change recorded.
Can you manage my account directly?
Where it is needed, access is granted at a level you choose, and you can remove it at any time. Every change is logged so you know what has been done.

Results and expectations

Can you guarantee sales, bestseller status or reviews?
No, and I would be wary of anyone who did. Those outcomes depend on many factors outside any strategist's control. I promise clear analysis, a written plan and honest reporting.
How soon will I see results?
It varies. Listing changes can take time to be reflected in search and browse. Promotions and ads can show early signals, but a reliable reading usually needs a few weeks of data.
What if my book is already selling well?
That is a good starting point. The audit can still show where a stronger category, a sharper keyword set or a better launch rhythm could help your next release or your backlist.
Will I receive a report?
Yes. Every package includes a written report, delivered as a PDF with an editable DOCX copy, plus follow-up questions during the agreed period.

Is this for me?

Is this right for a debut author?
Yes. Debut authors often benefit most from getting categories, keywords and launch timing right before release. Setup and Launch is designed with that in mind.
Is this right for an author with a backlist?
Yes. Many backlist titles are still discoverable with better categories, keywords, blurbs and covers. Full Service Partnership suits series and backlist authors who want ongoing support.
What if I only want advice on my categories?
Audit Only covers category analysis in depth, and you can ask for a focused review of one area. Tell me what you need in your message.
What if I am not sure which package I need?
Take the Book Visibility Score, then request a free audit. I will recommend the option that fits, and you are free to choose a different one.
