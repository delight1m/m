Home / The Challenge

The Visibility Challenge

Most authors do not have a quality problem. They have a visibility problem. A book can be exceptional and still stay invisible to the systems and readers meant to discover it. Here is how to tell if that is your situation, and what is causing it.

Why Good Books Stay Invisible

Every day, countless well-written, professionally edited and beautifully covered books launch into silence. Their authors did everything "right": a real editor, a real cover, a story readers genuinely enjoy. Months later they are still asking why nobody is buying.

Here is the uncomfortable truth. Readers are not rejecting these books. They are simply never seeing them.

Online bookstores hold millions of titles. In that ocean, your book is one drop, invisible by default unless you build the structure that places it in front of the right reader at the right moment.

Visibility is not about shouting louder. It is about standing exactly where your ideal reader is already looking.

The Core Insight

The gap between a book that sells 50 copies and one that sells 5,000 is rarely writing quality. Both can be equally well crafted. It is almost always positioning: whether the book appears where its natural readers browse.

Understanding The Gap

What is a visibility gap?

A visibility gap is the distance between where your book sits today and where your ideal reader is actually looking. Picture that reader scrolling a bookstore, typing search terms, browsing categories, scanning "also bought" lists and checking BookTok for their next read. If your book never enters that path, the gap exists, and it widens the longer it is ignored.

It is rarely one clean break. It is usually several small misalignments stacking up. Every one is measurable and every one is fixable.

  • Category drift: you are filed under "Contemporary Fiction" while your readers browse "Small Town Romance."
  • Keyword blindness: your metadata targets terms nobody actually types.
  • Also-bought decay: your recommendation list drifts toward books that share nothing with yours.
  • Pricing mismatch: your price signals the wrong genre or quality tier.
  • Blurb confusion: your description misses the genre promise readers scan for.
  • Platform absence: you are not where your readers gather.
  • Community disconnect: the book clubs and influencers who would champion you do not know you exist.

07

Usually a prioritization problem, not an effort problem. Most authors need to hear what to ignore as much as what to do.

08

"Money in, no results out"

Why traditional marketing often fails

Most book marketing advice is about promotion: get the word out, build buzz, make noise. But promotion without positioning is turning up the volume on static. Energy and money go into broadcasting to people who were never going to buy.

Authors who come to me have often already tried ads that burned budget, newsletter swaps that brought subscribers who never bought, steady social posting with no link to sales, and publicity that never converted. The tactics were not necessarily wrong, but tactics without strategy are just activity, and activity is not progress.

Start Here

The foundation has to come first

Before any promotional tactic can work, these six things must be in place. Once they are, ads reach people who were already looking, social posts land because you are speaking their language, and the algorithm begins working for you.

  • Correct category placement: specific subcategories your readers actually shop.
  • Aligned keywords: matched to real reader searches, not guesses.
  • A truthful, fast blurb: readers know within seconds if the book is for them.
  • Pricing that signals the right tier: neither too high nor too low.
  • Presence where readers gather: this varies enormously by genre.
  • Clear comp titles: know where you sit beside books your readers already love.
The Bottom Line

Every visibility problem is fixable

None of it is about talent, luck or connections. It is structure and positioning, and both can be analyzed, measured and repaired step by step.

My Story

I noticed what others overlooked

I did not plan to become a book marketing specialist. Like many people in this industry, I arrived by noticing patterns that others seemed to miss.

It started with a question I could not put down: why do some books with excellent reviews and devoted readers stay completely unseen, while others with seemingly weaker content find their audience?

The easy answer was "marketing," but that felt too simple. Plenty of authors were running ads and posting daily, and many still struggled while others succeeded almost effortlessly.

So I studied the difference. I examined rank patterns, category placements, keyword strategies, review speed, recommendation lists and pricing. Slowly, one answer kept returning: positioning. Visible books were placed where their natural readers could find them. Invisible books sat in the wrong categories, aimed at the wrong keywords, making discovery nearly impossible.
